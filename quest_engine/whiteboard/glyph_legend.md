# What The Lights Mean

The children have no screen. These twenty-five red lights and two beeps are the
whole interface, so this chart earns its place on the whiteboard.

Key for the diagrams below: `#` bright, `o` dim, `.` off.

---

## Write this on the board

```
     WHAT YOUR LIGHTS MEAN

  one dot, blinking slowly  ->  waiting for the story
  one dim dot in a corner   ->  NOT YOUR TURN
  dots along the top        ->  how many you have done
  blob growing from middle  ->  KEEP HOLDING
  one dot wandering about   ->  tip it to centre the dot
  arrow                     ->  turn that way
  big tick                  ->  you did it
  big cross                 ->  you missed it
  hollow square             ->  tell a grown-up (not your fault)
```

---

## The full set

### Waiting — slow blink, one dim centre dot

```
. . . . .
. . . . .
. . o . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
```
The wand is on, connected and waiting for the next instruction.

### Not your turn — one dim corner dot

```
o . . . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
```
Someone else was called out by name. This exists so a child can tell the
difference between "not for me" and "my wand is broken", which they cannot
otherwise do without a screen.

### Counting — slots along the top row

Two of three presses done:

```
# # o . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
```
Bright means done, dim means still to go. Used for presses, claps, shakes and
tilts.

### Holding — blob growing out of the middle

About half way through a hold:

```
. . . . .
. # # # .
. # # # .
. # # # .
. . . . .
```
Fills from the centre outwards as they hold. If they let go it drops back to
nothing and starts again. Used for HOLD and SHOUT.

### Spirit level — one dot to steer to the middle

Tipped towards the bottom right:

```
. . . . .
. . . . .
. . . . .
. . . # .
. . . . .
```
The dot brightens when it is inside the tolerance. Used for FLAT AND STILL.

### Turn that way — an arrow

```
. . # . .        . . # . .
. # . . .        . . . # .
# # # # #        # # # # #
. # . . .        . . . # .
. . # . .        . . # . .
   turn left        turn right
```
Used for POINT. It becomes a tick when they are on the right bearing.

### You did it — tick, plus a rising beep

```
. . . . #
. . . # .
# . # . .
. # . . .
. . . . .
```

### You missed it — cross, plus a falling beep

```
# . . . #
. # . # .
. . # . .
. # . # .
# . . . #
```

### Hollow box — a grown-up problem, not the child's fault

```
# # # # #
# . . . #
# . . . #
# . . . #
# # # # #
```
Deliberately unlike the tick and the cross. It means the wand was asked for a
compass bearing but its compass was never calibrated, so the child could not
have succeeded whatever they did. Tell them it is not their fault, skip the step,
and see the compass section of the README.

## The two beeps

| Sound | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Two notes going **up** | it worked |
| Two notes going **down** | it did not |

The beeps matter more than they look. A child in the middle of a hall cannot see
your laptop, so their own wand has to be the thing that tells them whether they
succeeded. If the wands are inaudible over the narration, turn the PA down before
you turn anything else up.
